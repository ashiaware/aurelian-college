Tuition & aid

One price. Everything included.

Tuition ($22,000), fees ($800), housing, meals, a bus pass, and every supply your major needs. Your price is locked for four years, and families earning under $75,000 pay no tuition at all.

Studio tiers

Studios never close

Every package includes 24-hour studio access with your House card. Tiers add extra lessons and reserved space, not hours.

EncoreIncluded

Every studio, practice room, and lab open to you 24 hours a day, seven days a week, plus the weekly tutorial and private lessons your major already includes.

Beyond the bill

Help that isn't on the price tag

Lower-income students often struggle with costs nobody mentions. These cover them.

No-loan pledgeUnder $110,000

Families earning under $110,000 get aid packages with no loans at all. Above that, loans are capped at $3,500 a year, the federal first-year limit.

First-generation and starter grants$1,500 once

First in your family to go to college? You get a $1,500 Starter Grant for bedding, a winter coat, and travel home, and a peer mentor from your House.

Free to apply and auditionAlways free

No application fee for anyone. Video auditions are always free, and families under $75,000 get the Travel Grant below for in-person auditions and Visit Day.

The Travel Grant: getting here, and getting homeUp to $5,000 a year

Families earning under $75,000 get a Travel Grant of up to $5,000 a year for flights, trains, buses, and checked bags. It pays for travel to your audition or Visit Day, move-in, and trips home at winter break and the end of the year. You book, and the Bursary reimburses you within a week, or we can book the tickets for you so you never pay up front.

Once you're here, the rest is handled. Your room and meals are part of your all-inclusive package, and your Promise grant pays for them: in full under $40,000, and down to at most $3,500 a year (which work-study covers) up to $75,000. Every student also gets a free unlimited subway and bus card for the year.

A laptop and your kitYours to keep

Every Promise student under $75,000 gets a laptop with the software for their major. Every student gets the full supply kit: tools, metals, clay, fabric, paint, and shoes.

The Emergency FundUp to $1,000

A broken instrument, a flight home for a family emergency, a sudden medical bill: the Senior Tutor can grant up to $1,000 within 48 hours. No repayment.

Food and the PantryFree

The Hearth Pantry in Ashcombe Hall is free and anonymous, and the Refectory gives away unused meal swipes donated by other students.

      In this order

      How your aid is put together

      You get one aid letter on March 31, with your decision. Every source is added in this order, and nothing is ever taken back to make room for another.

      1. Federal and state grants

        Pell and state grants come first, from your FAFSA.

      2. The Aurelian Promise

        Our grant fills the gap down to your family's Promise cap.

      3. Merit scholarship

        $2,000 to $8,000 a year, decided at your audition or portfolio review. It lowers what you pay, even on the Promise.

      4. Named scholarship

        One of 64 donor scholarships for your art, chosen automatically. It also lowers what you pay.

      5. Work-study

        Up to $3,000 a year from a campus job, paid to you or straight to your bill.

      No extra forms

      Scholarships, and how you get them

      Aurelian has two kinds: merit scholarships, set by how your audition or portfolio is scored, and 15 named scholarships, one for each art plus one for first-generation students. Every applicant is considered for both automatically.

      1. Apply and tick one box

        On your application, keep “Consider me for merit and named scholarships” ticked. There are no essays or extra forms.

      2. Show your work

        Three faculty score your audition, portfolio, or writing out of 100 on craft, potential, voice, and commitment. That score sets your merit award.

      3. Your House's committee meets

        In early March, each House's scholarship committee reads every scored applicant in its art and chooses the named scholarship winners.

      4. It's all in your letter

        On March 31 your decision letter lists every award you won. You don't need to accept them separately.

      Scores are never affected by your family's income. A Promise student with a 92 gets the same $8,000 as anyone else, and it lowers what they pay.

      Who decides named scholarships?House committee

      Each House has a scholarship committee: the Head of House, two Fellows of that art, and a student prefect. Donors can meet the winners but never choose them or see applications.

      Can I win more than one?They stack

      Yes. Merit, one named scholarship for your art, and the First-Generation Scholarship can all stack, on top of the Promise. They only ever lower your price, down to $0. If they add up to more than your price, the extra becomes a living allowance.

      Do I need the FAFSA?Only for need-based aid

      Not for merit or named scholarships. You do need it for the Aurelian Promise and federal grants. International students send the Aurelian aid form instead.

      How do I keep them?Renews yearly

      Every award renews for all four years as long as you pass your juries or portfolio reviews and stay in good standing. There is no grade average to maintain. A Not Yet grade never costs you a scholarship on its own.

      Can I apply again later?From second year

      Yes. After first year, students can be put forward for upper-year scholarships by their tutor, including the Quartet Prize and the Senior Showcase awards.

      What about international and transfer students?Same rules

      They are considered for every merit and named scholarship in the same way. Transfer students are scored on their audition or portfolio like everyone else.

      Financial aid

      Seven in ten students get aid. Most combine two or three of these, and all of it comes off the same single price.
