Campus life

Accessibility

Our buildings are old, but getting into them, training in them, and living in them shouldn't be hard. Accommodations are free, private, and planned with you.

Accessibility Services, Ashcombe Hall, ground floor. Weekdays 9am to 5pm. access@aurelianarts.edu

Four steps

Getting accommodations

  1. 1

    Register with Accessibility Services

    Fill in a short online form, any time: before you arrive, in your first week, or halfway through your degree.

  2. 2

    Share what you have

    A letter from a doctor, therapist, or school helps, but it isn't required to get started. We can begin with temporary accommodations while you gather it.

  3. 3

    Meet your counselor

    A 30-minute conversation, in person or by video, about how you learn, train, and live. We plan together; nothing is decided for you.

  4. 4

    Your Fellows get a letter

    We send an accommodation letter to your Fellows and Head of House. It says what you need, never why.

Every light belongs

What we can arrange

A few of the things students use most. If you need something that isn't here, ask; most plans are built one person at a time.

Classes and juries

  • Extra time or a quiet room for exams and written juries
  • Alternative formats for critiques and reviews
  • Captioned lectures and audio-described slides
  • Note-taking support and recorded seminars

Studios and stages

  • Adapted technique classes and an adapted aerial program
  • Seated soldering stations at the Forge and an accessible wheel in the Kiln House
  • Height-adjustable drafting and cutting tables
  • Assisted rigging, ramps backstage, and accessible dressing rooms

Living and dining

  • An accessible version of every room type, at the same price
  • Quiet, sensory-friendly floors and rooms for service and support animals
  • Reachable counters, tray service, and plans for medical diets
  • A personal emergency plan with the Porter's Lodge

Getting around

  • Step-free routes into every historic building
  • Free campus shuttle and paratransit until 2am
  • Accessible parking beside each House wing
  • Accessible transport on museum and study trips

Technology

  • Screen readers, magnifiers, and dictation software in the Library
  • Accessible music notation and captioning tools
  • Loaner laptops and tablets with assistive apps
  • Training from our assistive-tech specialist

Temporary injuries

  • Dancers and circus artists get injured. We arrange lifts, rides, and modified classes the same day
  • Adjusted jury dates while you recover
  • A plan with the Physio Room to get you back safely
  • No documentation needed for the first two weeks

Before you enroll

Applying and auditioning

You can ask for accommodations for your audition, portfolio review, or interview: extra time, a seated or adapted audition, an accessible studio, an interpreter, or a video audition instead of coming to campus.

Carmen Ibarra, our accessibility counselor in Admissions, plans it with you and passes your plan to Accessibility Services if you enroll, so you only explain once.

Common questions

Questions

Will asking for accommodations affect my admission?Never

No. Admissions decisions are made before anyone sees an accommodation request, and the people who plan your audition accommodations are not on the faculty panel.

What does it cost?Free

Nothing. Every accommodation, assistive technology, and accessible room is free, and accessible rooms cost the same as any other room of their type.

Who finds out about my disability?Private

Only Accessibility Services. Your Fellows and Head of House get a letter listing your accommodations, never your diagnosis. You choose whether to tell anyone else.

Can I bring a service or support animal?Yes

Yes. Service animals are welcome everywhere on campus. Emotional support animals can live in your room; tell us by June 1 so we can place you on the right floor.

I didn't ask for anything in first year. Is it too late?Any time

It is never too late. Many students register in their second or third year, often when the work gets more physical or the reading gets heavier.

This website

Website accessibility

We aim for this website to meet the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.1 at level AA. Every page works with a keyboard, has visible focus, respects reduced motion, and has a light and dark mode.

If something doesn't work for you, tell us at access@aurelianarts.edu and we will send the information another way while we fix it.
