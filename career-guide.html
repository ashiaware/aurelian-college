How to get an internship or job

A plain guide from the Stage Door Office: what to do each year, what to prepare for your field, and how to apply.

Questions? Tamsin Clarke runs the Stage Door Office. stagedoor@aurelianarts.edu

You don't have to do it all at once

Year by year

First year

Get a campus job and book one Stage Door visit

Work a few shifts in the box office, the Forge, or the Library. Meet your Stage Door advisor once, even for twenty minutes, so they know your name and what you make.

Second year

Build your materials and try a short placement

Get your first real headshots, reel, or portfolio shoot (free through the office). Apply for a winter or two-week placement to see how a company works.

Third year

Do a full summer internship

Most students do their main internship the summer after third year. Applications open in November and most close by February.

Fourth year

Showcase, then apply for jobs

Your House showcase puts your work in front of agents, galleries, and studios. Start applying for jobs in January; the office reads every cover letter before it goes out.

For any internship or job

Six steps

  1. 1

    Book a Stage Door appointment

    Thirty minutes with an advisor who knows your field. Bring whatever you have, even if it's rough. Book online or drop in at the stage door, weekdays 10am to 6pm.

  2. 2

    Get your materials ready

    Every field needs something different (see below). Your advisor and a Fellow from your House review it with you before anything goes out.

  3. 3

    Find openings

    Check the internship list, ask your Quartet mentor, and come to Industry Night in October, when 40 companies and studios come to campus.

  4. 4

    Apply

    Most applications need a résumé, a short letter, and your work sample. Send the letter to stagedoor@aurelianarts.edu first; we reply within two working days.

  5. 5

    Prepare for the audition or interview

    Book a mock audition or mock interview with someone from the industry. Practice talking about one piece of your work for two minutes.

  6. 6

    Say yes, and sort out the details

    Get up to 3 elective credits, apply for the Stage Door Stipend if it's unpaid, and tell your Head of House so juries can move if needed.

Your work sample

What to prepare for your field

Pick your area to see what companies, studios, and galleries will ask for.

  • Headshot

    A clear, recent photo that looks like you on a normal day. The office runs free headshot days every term.

  • Performance résumé

    One page: height, roles, training, special skills. Not the same as an office résumé.

  • Reel or self-tape

    Two minutes at most. Lead with your strongest 20 seconds.

  • Two contrasting pieces

    Two monologues, songs, or dance solos, ready to perform at any time.

  • Recordings

    Three short pieces recorded in the Chapel or the Listening Room, in different styles.

  • Repertoire list

    Everything you could play or conduct tomorrow, in one document.

  • Scores (composers)

    Two clean scores with a recording of each, even a MIDI one.

  • Short bio

    Fifty words and a hundred words. Programs will ask for both.

  • Reel

    Ninety seconds of your best shots, edits, or designs. Put your name and role on screen.

  • Credits list

    Every production you worked on and what you did on it.

  • Portfolio site

    One page with your reel, three projects, and how to reach you.

  • References

    Two people who saw you work: a Fellow and a production lead.

  • Portfolio

    Ten to fifteen pieces, best first. Show process for two of them.

  • Artist statement

    One short paragraph about what you make and why, in plain words.

  • CV

    Shows, awards, and residencies, plus where you trained.

  • Photos of your work

    Shot well and in good light. Book the photo studio in the Library for free.

Money, credit, and visas

Pay and credit

What if the internship is unpaid?Up to $4,500

Apply for the Stage Door Stipend: up to $4,500 for a summer internship, so nobody has to turn down a good placement because it doesn't pay. Students with the Aurelian Promise are funded first. Ashcombe Hall stays open over the summer at no cost if your internship is in New York.

Do internships count for credit?Up to 3 credits

Yes. A summer internship of at least 120 hours earns up to 3 elective credits. In Arts Management a third-year internship is required.

Can I find my own internship?Yes

Yes, and many students do. Bring the offer to the Stage Door Office and we'll check it, set up credit, and help with the stipend.

I'm an international student. Can I take an internship?Yes, through CPT

Yes. Off-campus internships count as Curricular Practical Training (CPT). The International Office sets it up with you before you start; it usually takes a week. More for international students.

What if I don't get anything?

It happens to almost everyone at least once. Your advisor will go through what you sent with you and find a second round of openings. Plenty of placements open in March and April.
