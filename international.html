International students

Students from 31 countries live and train at Aurelian. Here's how to apply, pay for it, get your visa, and land in New York with someone waiting for you.

Your advisor is Sofia Lindqvist-Reyes, Advisor for International & Transfer Students. s.lindqvist@aurelianarts.edu · (555) 014-2205

From application to arrival

Six steps to New York

  1. 1

    Apply by January 15

    The same application as everyone else, free. Audition or send a portfolio online from anywhere; video auditions count the same as in-person ones.

  2. 2

    Tell us about your English

    There's no minimum score. Send a TOEFL, IELTS, or Duolingo result if you have one, or skip the test and have a relaxed 20-minute video chat with Sofia. It only helps us plan your English support.

  3. 3

    Apply for aid

    International students can get the Aurelian Promise. Fill in the Aurelian International Aid Form by February 15 instead of the FAFSA, with your family's income in any currency.

  4. 4

    Get your I-20

    After you say yes on May 1, we issue your Form I-20, the document you need for an F-1 student visa. We help you show proof of funds, and your aid counts toward it.

  5. 5

    Book your visa interview

    Pay the SEVIS fee, fill in the DS-160, and book an interview at the U.S. embassy or consulate near you. Book early; summer waits can be long.

  6. 6

    Fly in for International Welcome

    Arrive on September 21, six days before everyone else. We meet you at JFK, LaGuardia, or Newark and bring you to Ashcombe Hall.

Free for every student

English support

Your art matters more than your accent. If English is your second (or third) language, we help you every step of the way, and it never costs extra. Book English tutoring.

English for the Arts, free

A four-week summer course in New York before your first term, with housing and meals included. You learn the words of your art (rehearsal notes, critiques, contracts) and settle in early.

Weekly tutoring

One-on-one sessions with an English tutor all year, as many as you want, at no cost. Bring an essay, a script, or a scene.

The Writing & Speaking Center

Help with papers, artist statements, and practice for juries and interviews, open evenings in the Library.

Conversation partners

We pair you with a student from your House for coffee and practice twice a week. Most pairs become friends.

Extra time and support in class

Extra time on written exams and your choice of a dictionary in class. Your Fellow can give critiques in writing as well as out loud.

Help in your language

Orientation guides in eight languages, and counselors who speak Spanish, Mandarin, Korean, French, and Portuguese.

Questions we hear most

Visas, work, and life

The International Office in the Porter's Lodge helps with every form, from your I-20 to your first tax return.

Can international students get financial aid?Yes

Yes. Aid works the same for every student we admit, wherever they're from. The Aurelian Promise income tiers work the same way, converted from your currency, and every named scholarship is open to you.

Who pays for flights?Up to $5,000 a year

Families earning under $75,000 (converted from your currency) get the Travel Grant, up to $5,000 a year. For international students it covers your flight in at the start of the year and home again in June, and a winter flight home if the grant has room.

Can I work while I study?20 hours a week

Yes, on campus. F-1 students can work up to 20 hours a week during term and full time over breaks. Every campus job on the Jobs page is open to you. Internships off campus count as Curricular Practical Training (CPT), which the International Office arranges.

What about after I graduate?Up to 12 months

F-1 graduates can apply for up to 12 months of Optional Practical Training (OPT) to work in their field in the U.S. The Stage Door Office runs an OPT workshop every Spring for fourth-years.

Where do I live over breaks?Open all year

Ashcombe Hall stays open over every break at no extra cost, and the Refectory serves Thanksgiving and Lunar New Year dinners for everyone who stays.

Health insuranceIncluded

Every student is covered by the College health plan, which is part of your all-inclusive price. The Wellbeing Centre has counselors who speak Spanish, Mandarin, Korean, French, and Portuguese.

Phones, banks, and moneyWeek one

During International Welcome we take you to open a U.S. bank account, get a phone plan, and set up your free subway card. Your Promise grant and work-study pay go straight into your student account.

Online audition days

Audition from your time zone

Live online auditions run on Saturdays at 9am and 7pm New York time, so there's a slot that isn't the middle of the night for you.

Times are for winter auditions (January and February), when New York is on Eastern Standard Time.
